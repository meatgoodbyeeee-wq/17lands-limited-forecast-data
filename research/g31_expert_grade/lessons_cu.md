# Lessons: ranking commons and uncommons within a colour before release

Source: 11 past sets, about 2,200 commons and uncommons. Each card has an expert pre-release grade (0-5) and its actual GIH win rate minus the set mean (pp).

## Calibration of the expert ordering

1. **The ordering is right on average but compressed, and noisy at the card level.** Within a colour, grade and result correlate at about 0.5. Average result by grade: 1.0 → -4.0pp, 1.5 → -2.2, 2.0 → -0.7, 2.5 → +0.5, 3.0 → +1.2, 3.5 → +2.1. That is about +1.2pp per half grade, flattening at the top. Within a single grade the SD is about 2.4pp, so a "2.0" can land anywhere from -5 to +4. Use the grade as a prior, and let card-type rules below move a card by up to two grade steps.
2. **"Unplayable" calls are reliable. "Premium" calls are not.** Cards graded 1.0 or lower almost always finish -3 to -11pp. Cards graded 3.0-3.5 often finish near 0, especially conditional removal, build-arounds and synergy payoffs. Treat a high grade as reliable only when the card is simple: removal or a two-for-one.
3. **Uncommons spread wider than commons.** Bad uncommons (narrow build-arounds, gimmicks) are worse than bad commons, about -4.6 against -2.9 at grade 1.0. Good uncommons are slightly better. Push uncommon estimates further from the middle than common estimates.

## Card types that beat their grade

4. **Two-for-one creatures are the most underrated group.** Examples: a body plus a token, a clue/map/land-fetch token, "look at two, keep one", a cantrip, or a death trigger that replaces itself. They typically finish +2 to +5pp while graded only 2.0-2.5. Cheap ones (1-3 mana) are best, at +3 to +7. Rank every common of this type in the top third of its colour.
5. **Removal on a creature beats the same removal on a spell.** Examples: ETB exile-until-leaves, ETB destroy, flash ETB -X/-X, ETB bite or fight, or a removal effect stapled to a vehicle. These finish +3 to +6pp, about 1-2pp above comparable spell removal. They are the best uncommons in most colours.
6. **"Exile until this leaves" enchantments and pacifism-style auras are solid but not premium:** +2 to +4pp in most sets, and about 0 in sets with abundant enchantment hate or bounce. A version that adds a body or token reached +6.
7. **Cheap evasive bodies with small value are badly underrated.** 1-mana fliers with lifelink, 1/1 fliers that make a token or explore, and 2-mana fliers with upside go +3 to +7pp at grades of 2.0-3.0. Ground 1-drops and vanilla 1-drops sit at -1 to -3.
8. **Green ramp-on-a-body and efficient big green creatures beat their grade.** Examples: a 3-drop that fetches a land, a mana dork, a 6-7 mana fatty with ETB value or ward, or a 5-mana body with outsized stats. These go +2 to +6pp at grades of 2.0-2.5. In contrast, ramp sorceries with no body are -4 to -6, and vanilla stats with no evasion or value cap out around 0 to -2.
9. **Blue card selection on a creature or permanent beats raw card draw.** "Look at N, keep one" creatures, tutors for the colour's key card type, and cantrip artifacts that replace themselves go +3 to +5pp. Plain "draw two/three" spells sit at -3 to +1, and "discard a card, draw two" at -2 to -4.

## Card types that miss their grade

10. **Conditional and threshold payoffs are systematically overrated, by 2-4pp.** This covers anything that works only if a graveyard count, "second spell this turn", "you gained life this turn", "two tapped creatures", "a noncreature spell was cast" or a puzzle condition is met. Commons with weak base stats that wait for an enabler routinely finish -3 to -7. Evaluate the card as if the condition is off half the time.
11. **Spells-matter payoffs on bodies are among the worst commons in every set.** Prowess-style creatures, "whenever you cast a noncreature spell" pingers, and cost reducers for instants/sorceries finish -3 to -6pp, even when the set clearly supports the archetype. Limited decks don't run enough spells.
12. **Combat tricks default to -1 to -4pp.** The exceptions leave something behind: a +1/+1 counter, a clue, a token, or protection plus a counter. Those reach 0 to +3. Instant-speed team pumps are -1 to -4 outside a strongly go-wide set. Anything that grants double strike (trick or aura) is -3.5 to -8.
13. **Self-buff auras and almost all equipment underperform.** Auras on your own creature go -1 to -6 unless they have flash and a cantrip. Colourless equipment goes -2 to -11pp; experts grade it low already, but not low enough. Equipment that makes its own body is the exception, at about 0 to +2.
14. **Small symmetric sweepers are traps,** at -2 to -6pp (e.g. -2/-2 to all creatures, 2 damage to each creature, each player sacrifices). Experts sometimes grade them 2.0-3.0. One-sided small sweepers are fine (+2 to +3), especially as a mode on a flexible card.
15. **Removal that compensates the opponent underperforms.** Examples: destroy and they get a token, a face-down body, a land, a card or a food. These go -1.5 to -7pp even at instant speed and in premium colours. Rank such cards like a mediocre trick, not like real removal.
16. **Direct-damage-to-player, life drain and pure lifegain cards are near the bottom,** at -4 to -10pp. Lifelink on a cheap evasive body is the exception (see 7).
17. **Walls, defenders, "can't block" bodies, 0-power utility creatures and graveyard-hate bodies are -2 to -7pp.** Experts grade them 1.0-1.5, which is roughly right, but they still land below that grade's average.
18. **Mechanic-dependent permanents with no immediate effect are bad.** Vehicles, mounts or spacecraft that need crew/saddle/charge, crew-helper creatures, and mana sinks that pay off only later go -2 to -6pp. The same chassis with a meaningful ETB effect (exile, draw two, regrowth, land) goes +1 to +6. Value a set mechanic by what the card does the turn it is cast.

## Set and colour context

19. **Removal density decides what removal is worth.** In colours or sets with plenty of cheap removal and two-for-one creatures, a plain 3-mana "destroy target creature" common averaged only about 0pp, though graded 2.5-3.0. Cheap 1-2 mana removal stayed +2 to +4. Where removal is scarce (green bite/fight), even conditional bites go +2 to +6. Rank removal by speed and by how much the colour already has.
20. **Damage-based removal loses value as creature sizes grow.** In sets with many 4-toughness commons, 3-damage burn and land-count burn slid to about 0 to -3pp. 4-5 damage for 1-2 mana stayed +2 to +4. Expensive removal at 5-6 mana is -1 to -3 unless it has a discount condition, such as a tapped target, which brings it to +1 to +3.
21. **Average strength differs by colour, and gold uncommons are the strongest group.** Average results against the set mean: multicolour +0.4, white -0.2, green -0.2, blue -0.6, black -0.9, red -1.3, colourless/lands -1.5. In a typical set, red's non-removal filler (haste dorks, aggressive 2-3 drops, looters) should start about 1pp below the same grade in other colours.
22. **Among gold uncommons, cheap value creatures dominate.** Value creatures at 2-3 mana and gold removal finish +3 to +6pp, which justifies grades of 3.0-3.5. Gold build-arounds that need heavy density, card-type counts or a narrow trigger finish -2 to -4 despite similar grades. Within gold, rank by stand-alone value.
23. **The colour's archetypes matter only where the card is good on its own.** In go-wide sets, "make three 1/1s" and repeatable token makers rose to +3 to +6. In sets where a colour's archetype was graveyard-count or spells-matter, that colour's commons were mostly negative. A colour whose commons mostly serve one demanding archetype should be ranked lower overall.
24. **Format speed shifts the mana curve.** In tempo/aggressive sets, efficient 2-drops with any upside are +2 to +4, and 6-7 drops without immediate value are -2 to -4. In slower, bigger sets, top-end value creatures (7-mana 6/6 with value, flash fatties, ETB-damage dragons) reach +2 to +5. Bodies that are below the set's typical stats for their mana value lose 2-5pp, e.g. 2/2 for 3, enters-tapped 4/4 for 4, or 1/4 for 3 that needs a condition for power.

## Utility, lands and mana

25. **Lands and fixing.** Common dual taplands (gain-lands, cycling-lands) average about -0.5 ± 1pp; grade them as below-average playables. Duals with a relevant set-synergy rider were +1 to +2.5. Colourless utility lands with activated abilities, and type-restricted mana lands, are -2 to -8. Basic-land-search artifacts and bodies are worth +1 to +3 only in sets that push 3+ colours.
26. **Counterspells and bounce depend on the format.** Hard creature counters at 2 mana were +1.5 to +3.5 in slower sets. Narrow 1-mana noncreature counters were -5 to -6. Plain bounce instants sit near 0 to +1, and two-sided bounce at -2 to -3. Bounce on an ETB creature reaches +2 to +4.
27. **Hand attack and recursion only work on a body.** A plain 1-for-1 discard spell goes -0.1 to -6pp. An ETB discard 2-drop goes +1 to +2, and a "take a card until this leaves" creature reached +6. Return-to-hand recursion spells are mixed (-3 to +2). Reanimation that needs a sacrifice or other extra cost is -4 to -6, while recursion stapled to a creature or vehicle ETB is +2 to +5.
