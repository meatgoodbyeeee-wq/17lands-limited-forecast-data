# Lessons for an AI Limited grader (from AI-vs-expert disagreements)

Scale notes: "pp" = GIH win rate minus set mean. "AI pts" = points on the 0-10 AI scale. The expert compresses commons/uncommons toward the low-middle of their 0-5 scale and spreads rares wider, so their raw numbers are not 2x the AI's. What carries over is the *direction* of the corrections.

## A. Removal: the AI's largest systematic overrating

1. **Removal that hands the opponent something back is much worse than it reads.** "Destroy X, its controller gets a 2/2 / manifests / gets 2 tokens / a land-fetch token / a Clue" landed at -1.5 to -7.5 pp, about 3-6 pp below the model, even when it costs 1-2 mana or hits any nonland permanent. A 2/2 (or face-down 2/2) is a real card in Limited. The AI graded these 7-8. Grade them 4.5-6. Cheaper and wider targeting does not make up for it. A small token (a 1/1, or a Clue at 5 mana) is the mildest version but still costs about 1-2 pts.
2. **Clean, cheap, unconditional removal is still above average, so don't over-correct.** 2-3 MV "destroy/exile target creature" with no drawback landed at 0 to +4 pp, with residuals near 0 to +3. The expert sometimes graded these too low. 7-7.5 is right. Don't go higher, because "premium removal" at common is not a bomb.
3. **Expensive removal is filler.** 5-mana "6 damage" or -6/-6, 6-mana flash artifact damage, and 4-5 mana instant exile landed at -3.5 to +0.5 pp. Grade 5-5.5, not 6-6.5. Cycling or a rider adds almost nothing.
4. **Conditional removal is roughly break-even.** Attacking/blocking-only, tapped-only, power-4+-only, flier-only, or non-[type]-only landed at -1.5 to +1.5 pp (the narrow-type exclusion one was +3). Grade about 5. Being modal with an unrelated mode does not rescue it.
5. **"Loses all abilities / becomes a 1/1 / Food / land" auras are overrated by about 1.5-2.5 pts.** They landed at -6 to +0 pp. They leave a body, die to bounce or sacrifice synergies, and are weak when the set gives you things to do with your own creatures. Grade 4.5-5. Exceptions: a 1-mana version in a set where it also shuts off a key mechanic (+1.7), and a control-steal aura (+3.4, good).
6. **Edicts and "each player sacrifices" effects underperform in token-rich formats.** They landed at -0.5 to -6 pp. The bigger the format's token/go-wide density, the worse they get. A sacrifice-based mini-wrath on a legendary enchantment was -6.
7. **Sweepers at rare are underrated by the AI.** One-sided-ish or modal wraths, "destroy all + draw", and ETB board-clearers on big bodies came in at about +1 to +5 pp, with residuals mostly +1 to +7. The AI graded these 4-6. Grade them 6.5-7.5. Plain symmetric "destroy all, opponent with most creatures gets value" was mildly negative, so value attached to the wipe matters.

## B. Big creatures and top-end

8. **Common 6-drops with landcycling are overrated by about 1.5-2 pts.** These are big vanilla-ish bodies with basic-landcycling {2}. All of them landed at -0.8 to -4.1 pp. The flexibility is real, but the card mostly gets cycled. Grade 4-4.5, not 6-7.
9. **Mid-size common fliers (4-5 MV, 3/3 or 4/3 with a minor rider) are overrated by about 1-2 pts.** They landed at -5 to +0 pp. The AI's "evasion = good" prior is too strong in formats with reach/removal density. Only cheap evasive bodies with stacked keywords (flying + deathtouch + lifelink at 3) clearly beat average (+3.7).
10. **Expensive legendary rares whose power depends on a narrow engine flop.** Examples: draw on noncombat damage, sacrifice creatures to exile-and-cast, tap ten tribe members, "can cast spells via ward cost". These landed at -5 to -11 pp. The AI graded them 6-8.7 on raw stats. Grade 3-4 unless the body alone justifies the cost. Symmetric global drawbacks on the ETB (each player exiles most of their library) are disastrous (-11).
11. **But big fliers with an immediate effect are fine, and the expert underrated them.** Examples: a 6/6 flier at 7 with an ETB that blunts the opponent's attack, a 6/6 flash flier with a "can't lose" clause, a "sacrifice all others then reanimate" 8-drop. These landed at +3 to +3.6 pp. Keep them around 6-7. The expert's 1-2/5 was too low.
12. **Gold uncommon signposts at 4-7 MV with flashy text are overrated.** Examples: drain/draw on ETB or attack, deathtouch-team buffs, "whenever you attack, pump". They landed at -2.6 to +2.8 pp, with residuals of -0.5 to -4. Grade 5-5.5, not 7-8. Only the cheap ones with scaling team impact beat expectations.
13. **"Triggers an additional time" doublers and narrow legendary-matters payoffs underperform.** They landed at -0.8 to -3.4 pp. You rarely have enough of the subtype on board. Treat any "if a triggered ability of [subset] triggers" text as nearly blank.

## C. Synergy payoffs and engines: the AI's largest systematic underrating

14. **Cheap typal/archetype lords and growers are underrated by about 2-3 pts.** 1-3 MV rares/uncommons that buff, grow, or create tokens off the set's main creature types landed at +3 to +9 pp, with residuals of +2 to +7. The AI graded them 2.7-5.7. In sets built around those types (most draft decks will have 8-12 hits), grade them 6.5-8.
15. **Cheap card-advantage engines keyed to the set's core mechanic are strong.** Examples: draw on the set's enchantment/room trigger, artifact-entered card selection, ETB "look at top N for [set type]", unblockable looters, flash-into-draw. These landed at +4 to +7.5 pp. The AI graded them 3-4.7. If the trigger is something the set makes you do every turn, treat it as near-unconditional (+2-3 pts).
16. **Permanent team counters/tokens scale far better than temporary pumps.** X tokens + a counter on each, counters on each creature with flashback, second-spell token/counter triggers, and a land that puts a counter on each creature landed at +5.6 to +9.6 pp. The AI graded them 5-6. Grade them 7.5-8.5. By contrast a 5-mana gold one-shot Overrun was -2.6.
17. **Small scaling 1-drops at rare (increment, explore X, counters off allies, grow and ping) are underrated.** They landed at +3 to +5 pp. The AI graded them 2.7-4.3. Grade about 6.
18. **Mana creatures and fixers in multicolor or ramp sets are underrated by about 1.5-2 pts.** Any-color dorks, "lands and [type] tap for any color", and 2-drop dorks with upside landed at +1 to +6.5 pp. The more colors the set pushes, the bigger the effect.
19. **In a 3-color/wedge set, fixing and powerful 3-color spells beat expectations.** Expensive 3-color "do everything" instants/sorceries and converge payoffs landed at +5 to +13 pp. Fixing artifacts that fetch a basic and later make tokens came in at -2 to +3 pp, with residuals mostly positive. 3-color common filler at 6 MV was near average, not bad. The AI graded all of these 2-3 because of the color requirement. In a set designed for 3 colors, waive most of the multicolor penalty. Exception: 3-color gold cards with weak effects are still weak (-3).
20. **Graveyard-recursion and self-mill engines are underrated in graveyard-themed sets.** Examples: "cards leave your graveyard" payoffs, embalm/flashback grants, returning instants/sorceries, recursive creatures, "target player mills" on cheap bodies. These landed at +2 to +8 pp, with residuals of +4 to +12. The AI graded them 1-3. Don't read "target player mills" as pointless: it is self-fuel. Grade 5-6.5. Caveat: the set must actually reward the graveyard. Mill-for-the-sake-of-mill engines were -4.5 to -5.5 where it doesn't.
21. **Ramp/landfall value permanents are underrated.** Examples: a vehicle whose power equals your lands that fetches a land on ETB/attack, landfall enchantments that build to counters, prime-land token makers, and land-matters creatures. These landed at +6 to +10 pp. The AI graded them 4-5.

## D. Mechanics, costs and card types

22. **Vehicles with no ETB value and a real crew cost are overrated.** Evasive 3/3 or 4/4 vehicles with crew 1-2 and/or cycling landed at -4 to -5.5 pp. The AI graded them 5-6.7. Vehicles that pay off just by being cast (land search on ETB, explore the crewer, crew 1 with value) were +3 to +10. Value the ETB, not the stat line.
23. **Station/spacecraft-style "charge up" permanents: low threshold good, huge threshold bad.** A cheap first threshold with a team buff or an immediate ETB effect landed at +4 to +6 pp. A 20/20 that needs 10-20 counters landed at -6.6. Stations whose only reward is a 9+ evasive body are about average.
24. **Alt-cost/"cast it twice cheaply" commons (warp, plot, haste-for-a-turn) are overrated by about 1.5 pts.** Weak bodies with a warp or plot option landed at -2 to -7 pp. The AI graded them 5-5.7. The cheap mode is usually a tempo blip. Grade them on the hard-cast body.
25. **Speed / "max speed" rewards on non-aggressive cards are unreliable.** Card-draw artifacts, defensive fliers, and copy effects gated behind max speed landed at -1 to -7.3 pp. Cheap aggressive creatures that use speed (1-drop menace, a 2-mana indestructible attacker) were +3. Treat max speed as on only for aggressive low-curve cards.
26. **Combat tricks and pump instants at common/uncommon are below average.** Examples: +2 counters, "+1/+0 and draw when dies", "becomes X+1/X+1", blink-with-counter. They landed at -3 to -6.5 pp. Grade 3-4. Small multi-mode tricks that also drain or shrink, and cheap trample/artifact-hate tricks, were mildly positive (+2).
27. **Pure card draw / filtering spells without board impact are below average.** Examples: 4-mana "draw 2 lose 2", 5-mana "draw 3", 1-mana cantrip unblockable, "draw a card" fixing lessons. They landed at -2 to -5 pp. Grade about 3.5-4.
28. **Counterspells are neutral to bad.** Soft counters and noncreature-only counters landed at -5.7 to +2 pp. Grade about 3-4. Modal counter/top-or-bottom spells are about average.
29. **Common ETB "tap and stun" on 5-6 drops is filler.** It landed at about -1 to +0.5 pp. Grade about 5, not 6-6.7.
30. **Utility lands and taplands are near zero; the AI's ~2.5-3.5 is roughly right.** Exception: lands that turn into creatures or give team-wide counters/evasion landed at +3 to +5.6 pp. Grade those 5-6.
31. **Equipment that only grants an activated or attack-trigger utility effect is weak** (-5.5 to -1 pp). Equipment that makes its own body, or makes a body plus deathtouch/lifelink, can be strong (+7 for the latter). An expensive equipment with a "destroy target permanent" ETB was -4 because the body it needs isn't there.
32. **Weird or "unplayable-looking" rares are not 1/10.** Examples: draw on every spell or land drop with a life-total reset, a "first spell each turn is free" enchantment after refilling seven, symmetrical mill engines. They landed at about 0 to +4.5 pp. The AI's 1-2 was too low. Default to about 4-5 for strange but powerful rares unless the drawback is lethal.

## E. Where the expert was wrong (don't over-correct)

33. The expert overrated self-graveyard or "solve later" enchantments and slow engines. Examples: exile-on-attack card piles, death-return auras, cases that need 3 creatures dying in a turn, empty-library win conditions, an expensive "control opponent's turn" equipment, a 2-mana planeswalker you can't cast until turn 4. These landed at -4 to -8 pp despite expert grades of 3-4/5. Slow, setup-heavy noncreature engines need a body or immediate effect to be good.
34. The expert underrated efficient premium commons and uncommons, such as clean 2-3 MV kill spells, a flier with deathtouch and lifelink, a flash flier that exiles a creature or spell, and a mana-efficient 2-for-1 lifelink flier. These came in at +2.5 to +9.6 pp. Keep these high.

## What the expert used about the rest of the set

- **Speed of the format:** in fast, go-wide formats they downgraded slow 5-7 MV value bodies and edicts, and upgraded cheap growers and team buffs.
- **Removal density:** where cheap removal is plentiful, they discounted auras and slow engines that die to it, and discounted compensated removal (the trade is closer to even when the opponent's deck converts tokens into value).
- **Archetypes and creature types:** they asked how many cards in a typical deck trigger the payoff, and rated lords and engines high when the set is built around that type or mechanic (enchantments/rooms, artifacts, graveyard, landfall, typal pairs).
- **Color structure:** in wedge/3-color sets they valued fixing, monuments and 3-color bombs. In 2-color sets they penalized off-plan gold cards.
- **The mechanic's real reliability:** how often "max speed", "solve", station thresholds, or "second spell each turn" will actually be on in a normal game.
